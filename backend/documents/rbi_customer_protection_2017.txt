SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India
DOCUMENT: Customer Protection - Limiting Liability of Customers in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions
REFERENCE: DBR.No.Leg.BC.78/09.07.005/2017-18
DATE: July 6, 2017
JURISDICTION: India
APPLICABILITY: Scheduled Commercial Banks including RRBs, Small Finance Banks and Payments Banks

PURPOSE

The directions establish a framework for limiting customer liability in unauthorised electronic banking transactions and require banks to strengthen systems and procedures for customer protection.

ELECTRONIC BANKING SECURITY

Banks shall put in place appropriate systems and procedures to ensure the safety and security of electronic banking transactions carried out by customers.

Banks shall maintain robust and dynamic fraud detection and prevention mechanisms.

Banks shall assess risks arising from unauthorised transactions, measure liabilities arising from such events, and implement appropriate measures to mitigate those risks.

TRANSACTION ALERTS

Banks shall require customers to register for SMS alerts and, wherever available, email alerts for electronic banking transactions.

SMS alerts shall be sent to customers for electronic banking transactions.

REPORTING UNAUTHORISED TRANSACTIONS

Banks providing electronic banking services shall provide customers with 24x7 access through multiple channels for reporting unauthorised transactions and loss or theft of payment instruments.

Banks shall provide mechanisms allowing customers to report unauthorised transactions through channels such as website, phone banking, SMS, email, IVR and dedicated helplines.

Banks shall provide a direct link for reporting unauthorised electronic transactions on their website.

The reporting system shall provide an immediate acknowledgement of the complaint, including a registered complaint number.

Banks shall record the date and time of delivery of transaction alerts and customer responses.

IMMEDIATE PROTECTION

On receiving a report of an unauthorised transaction, banks shall take immediate steps to prevent further unauthorised transactions in the account.

CUSTOMER LIABILITY

Customer liability depends on the circumstances of the unauthorised transaction, including whether the incident resulted from bank negligence, customer negligence or a third-party breach.

Where the customer is negligent, such as by sharing payment credentials, the customer bears the loss until the transaction is reported to the bank. Loss occurring after reporting is borne by the bank.

For certain third-party breaches, customers may have zero liability when they notify the bank within the prescribed time.

REVERSAL AND COMPENSATION

For transactions qualifying for zero or limited liability, the bank shall provide shadow reversal of the amount involved within the prescribed period.

Banks shall establish procedures and timelines for resolving complaints and determining customer liability.

BOARD-APPROVED CUSTOMER PROTECTION POLICY

Banks shall formulate or revise a Board-approved customer relations policy covering customer rights and obligations for unauthorised transactions.

The policy should include customer awareness, procedures for reporting unauthorised transactions, complaint acknowledgement, grievance redressal, escalation procedures, resolution timelines and compensation.

BURDEN OF PROOF

The burden of proving customer liability in cases of unauthorised electronic banking transactions shall lie on the bank.

REPORTING AND MONITORING

Banks shall maintain mechanisms for reporting unauthorised electronic banking transaction cases to the Board or an appropriate Board Committee.

The framework also requires monitoring of such transactions and review by internal auditors.
