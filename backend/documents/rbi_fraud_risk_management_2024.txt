SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India
DOCUMENT: Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management in Commercial Banks (including Regional Rural Banks) and All India Financial Institutions
REFERENCE: RBI/DOS/2024-25/118
DATE: July 15, 2024
JURISDICTION: India
APPLICABILITY: Commercial Banks including RRBs, and All India Financial Institutions

PURPOSE

These Directions provide a framework to banks for prevention, early detection and timely reporting of incidents of fraud to Law Enforcement Agencies, the Reserve Bank of India and NABARD, along with related fraud risk management requirements.

FRAUD RISK GOVERNANCE

Banks shall have a Board-approved Fraud Risk Management Policy defining the roles and responsibilities of the Board, Board Committees and Senior Management.

Banks shall establish an appropriate organisational structure for fraud risk management within their overall risk management functions. A senior official of at least General Manager rank or equivalent shall be responsible for monitoring and reporting frauds.

EARLY WARNING SIGNALS AND RED FLAGGING

Banks shall maintain a framework for Early Warning Signals (EWS) and Red Flagging of Accounts (RFA) under the Board-approved Fraud Risk Management Policy.

A Red Flagged Account is an account where one or more Early Warning Signal indicators create suspicion of fraudulent activity and trigger deeper investigation and preventive measures.

The Risk Management Committee of the Board shall oversee the effectiveness of the EWS and RFA framework, while Senior Management is responsible for implementation.

INTERNAL CONTROLS AND AUDIT

Regulated entities are required to maintain robust internal audit and control frameworks as part of fraud risk management.

DATA ANALYTICS AND MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Banks shall use appropriate data analytics and market intelligence capabilities to strengthen fraud risk management and support early detection and prevention of fraud.

REPORTING

The framework includes requirements relating to reporting incidents of fraud to Law Enforcement Agencies and the Reserve Bank of India, as well as maintaining relevant fraud reporting information.

HUMAN OVERSIGHT

Fraud classification and related decisions must follow a defined governance process. The Directions require principles of natural justice to be followed in a time-bound manner before persons or entities are classified as fraudulent.
